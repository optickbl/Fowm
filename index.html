    Mistakes that kill brands

    Most young brands don't die from competitors. They die from a mistake they made themselves. Read this list before every big decision.

    • Restarting.Every new name, country or product puts you back at zero. Fowm, Athens and protein foam are now fixed until the numbers say otherwise.
    • Polishing before proving.Logos, packaging and websites don't matter until the foam tastes great and people reorder.
    • Splitting your time.15–30 hours a week is enough for Fowm, but only if Fowm gets most of it.
    • Pricing too low.At €24.90 you'd work for about €1.64 a pouch (Money). Cheap prices are very hard to raise later.
    • Big discounts.A 15% subscription discount or a 25% launch code can wipe out everything you keep.
    • Ads before reorders.Paying for strangers before your friends reorder burns cash fast.
    • Raising too much, too early.Super Coffee raised over $100M, then had to cut marketing by 80% and staff by 70% to survive (BevNET).
    • Going EU-wide on day one.Each country brings its own packaging registration, VAT and language rules (Legal).
    • Illegal claims."Fat burner", "boosts metabolism" or any health claim not on the EU list can get you fined or pulled.
    • Not owning your brand.No trademark or no written formula ownership means nothing to sell.
    • Copying another brand's look.Packaging that's too close to Grüns or anyone else invites legal letters. Stay clearly yourself.
    • One sales channel.Brands that only sell on Amazon are valued 1–2 points lower (Eightx).
    • Fake reviews or fake "was €X" prices.Both are illegal in the EU and destroy trust.
    • Ignoring cash.Whey prices swing, and stock eats cash before it makes any. Know your months of cash left, every week.

    The market and Fowm's edge

    Protein is the number one food trend of 2026, and cold foam is the fastest-growing way people add it to coffee. But almost nobody sells protein cold foam you make at home. That gap is Fowm.

    The wave is real

    • Innova ranked protein the number one food and drink trend of 2026. Nearly 60% of consumers worldwide say they're adding more protein to their diet (Tridge).
    • In Greece, protein milk sales grew 35.8% in a year, to €12.1M (Greek Dairy Conference 2026).
    • In Germany, high-protein sales grew 39.1% in a year, and 48% of shoppers buy high-protein foods (YouGov).
    • Not everywhere: most Italian consumers don't prioritise protein (FoodNavigator). Don't pick Italy as an early market.

    Starbucks already proved protein cold foam sells

    It launched Protein Cold Foam in the US and Canada on 29 September 2025, adding about 15g of protein to any cold drink. Cold foam was already used in one of every seven drinks (Starbucks). It became Starbucks' most popular protein add-on (Axios), and cold foam sales grew more than 40% in one quarter (Food Business News). In April 2026 it launched across Europe, the Middle East and Africa with 15g of whey (Protein Production Technology).

    What this means for you: the world's biggest coffee chain is teaching millions of people what protein cold foam is, for free. But you can only get it in their cafés, at their prices.

    Greece is the perfect test market

    • Greeks spent about €4B on coffee in 2024, €3.5B of it outside the home (Proto Thema).
    • Freddo espresso and freddo cappuccino make up 70–80% of all cold coffee drunk in Greece (To Vima). A freddo cappuccino is cold espresso with cold milk foam on top. Greeks already love the foam. Fowm just adds 15g of protein to it.
    • Prices are squeezing people: 45.7% of Greeks say they'll visit cafés less, and shoppers want products that recreate the café at home (Greek City Times). That's exactly what Fowm does.

    Making Fowm foam

    Fowm lives or dies on one test: does one scoop whip into thick foam in 20 seconds and taste great on a freddo? Nothing else matters until the answer is yes.

    How protein foam works

    • Foam is tiny air bubbles wrapped in protein. Frothing pushes air in. The protein spreads around each bubble like a skin and holds it up.
    • Whey isolate is the best foamer. In a patent test, a whey isolate foamed about as well as egg white. A whey concentrate with more fat and milk sugar foamed much worse (US patent 4218490).
    • Too much fat kills foam. A study of barista drinks found foam depends on protein level and on the balance of fat to protein (Omya). Your current test recipe has coconut milk powder, which is fat. Test a version without it.
    • Foam beats clumps. Starbucks first tried stirring protein straight into drinks, and it kept clumping. So they turned it into foam (Fast Company). That's your whole product story.
    • Hot coffee needs a helper. The dipotassium phosphate you ordered stops protein curdling in hot, acidic coffee.

    Your kitchen tests (do these first)

    You need: a handheld frother, a scale that reads 0.01g, cold milk, water, oat milk, freddo espresso and a timer. For 15g of protein you need about 17g of 90% whey isolate, so one serving is 20–25g of powder. That fits your 500g pouch with 20 servings.

    Log each version here as you test it. Try it with milk, water and oat milk. The winner's instructions become your pack's "how to use" line.

    Log a test to see which version is winning.

    How the leader is picked (my rule): no clumps, still foamy after 5 minutes, then the best taste. Ties go to the taller foam.

    Choosing a manufacturer

    Send one short brief to 5–8 manufacturers. Ask each for samples of your winning recipe.

    • Greece: AMHES Pharma does private label from formula to finished product, in small and large batches. It's already on your list.
    • EU, low minimums: ERA Scientifico in Latvia (white label from about 500 units), Nutrikal in Bulgaria (runs of 500–2,000 units), and Private Vitamin Lab in Slovenia, which does single-dose sachets (Wonnda).
    • Expect bigger minimums elsewhere. Branded protein powder often starts at 500–2,000 kg per order (Torg). That's 1,000–4,000 pouches, too many for a first test.

    Questions to ask every manufacturer:

    • What's your minimum order, and the price per kg at 250 kg, 500 kg and 1,000 kg?
    • How much are samples, and how fast?
    • Can you buy the whey isolate and fix its price for 6–12 months?
    • Which food-safety certificates do you hold (ISO 22000, FSSC 22000, HACCP)?
    • Will you test protein per serving and shelf life?
    • Can you pack sachets later, and at what minimum?
    • Do I own the formula? Get it in writing. A buyer will check this when you sell Fowm.

    Watch the whey price. Whey is your biggest cost, and it's at record highs. In April 2026, European whey isolate cost about €25,750 per tonne (Dairy Industries). Whey concentrate prices have about quadrupled in three years (Vesper). In October 2026, isolate was still trading near a record (Ingredients Network).

            How a brand becomes worth millions

            A company sells for its yearly profit (or yearly sales) times a number called the multiple. So to sell Fowm for millions, it has to make millions in sales, from customers who keep coming back every year.

            Sale price = yearly profit (or sales) × multiple

            The lemonade-stand version. Someone buys your lemonade stand because it makes money every year. If it makes €10k profit a year and they pay 3 years' worth, the stand sells for €30k. Nobody pays for a stand that has not sold any lemonade yet. That's why Fowm is worth €0 today, and why this whole plan is about sales that repeat.

            How buyers price a brand, by size

            • Small: under about €5M sales a year, run by you2.5–4.5 × yearly owner profit (Eightx). Example: €60k profit sells for €150–270k.
            • Medium: €5–15M sales, with a team3.5–5.5 × yearly profit (Eightx). Example: €1M profit sells for €3.5–5.5M.
            • Big and growing, bought by a food giant0.5–1.9 × yearly sales (Eightx). Example: €20M sales sells for €10–38M.
            • Hot brand, several giants competing to buy it3–4 × yearly sales or more (Grow). This is where billion-dollar deals come from.

            The big jump happens when a giant like Nestlé or PepsiCo buys you. They pay for your sales and your growth, not just your profit, because they can push your product into thousands of shops overnight.

            Timing matters too. Food deal prices dropped hard in 2025: the median food and drink takeover fell to 1.4 × sales, down from 1.9 × the year before (Eightx). You can't control the market, so build a brand that's worth buying in a bad year too.

            The ladder in one line. €1M in yearly sales makes Fowm worth roughly €0.3–1M. €10M makes it worth €5–19M. €100M makes it worth €50–190M or more. Billions need hundreds of millions in yearly sales, plus a bidding war.

            Montreal, the US and selling Fowm

            Prove Fowm in Greece, carry it to Canada when you move for university, and enter the US only once it's working. Plan the company structure 2–3 years before you sell, because Canada's best tax breaks need that long to qualify.

            The country path

            1. Greece, now to 2028Win Athens, then the rest of Greece.
            2. Canada, from fall 2027 if you moveCanadian labels need English and French, and your fluent French is a real edge in Montreal. Canada has its own rules for protein products, so hire a Canadian regulatory consultant before you sell there. Futurpreneur and the Dobson Cup become available once you live there (Grow).
            3. The US, laterIt's the biggest market, and Starbucks is already teaching Americans what protein cold foam is (Product). It's also the most competitive. Go once Canada proves the model.

            Tax: set it up years before you sell

            This is the most expensive place to make a mistake. Get a cross-border tax adviser for Greece and Canada before you move, and don't decide the structure alone.

            • Selling as a Greek resident15% tax on the gain from selling shares in a non-listed company (PwC).
            • Moving to Canada resets the clockWhen you become a Canadian resident, Canada treats most of what you own as bought at its value on the day you arrive. Growth from before you arrived generally isn't taxed in Canada (Barrett Tax Law).
            • Lifetime exemption (law today)Up to C$1,275,000 of gain tax-free in 2026 on qualifying shares (BDO). The company must run its business mainly in Canada and pass tests that look back 24 months (Money.ca). A Greek IKE wouldn't qualify.
            • Canada Growth Incentive (proposed, not law yet)The 5 November 2026 budget proposes making up to C$15 million of gain tax-free per person per business (Mintz). Under the proposal, the shares must be in a Canadian-controlled private company, issued from 5 November 2026 for cash or work, and held for 36 months. The business must run mainly in Canada for its first 24 months. Moving an existing business into a new company may not count. Product businesses appear to be included.
            • What this could mean for youIt might pay to start a fresh Canadian company for North America when you move, instead of moving the Greek one. That's a question for the adviser, not a decision to make from this page.

            How a sale actually happens

            1. 12–24 months beforeAudited accounts, written processes, every legal right in the company's name, and a growth story backed by numbers.
            2. Hire an adviserSmall online brands sell through brokers. Empire Flippers' deals ran 1–5 × yearly owner profit, with an average sale price of $270,802 in 2025 (Peony). Bigger brands use an investment bank that runs an auction.
            3. Offers come inYou pick one and sign a letter of intent: the price, the terms, and a promise not to talk to other buyers for a while.
            4. Due diligenceThe buyer checks everything: books, contracts, trademark, formula, customers.
            5. ClosingPart of the money is paid at once, and part later if targets are hit. PepsiCo's Poppi deal included extra payments for hitting sales goals after closing (NBC).
            6. You may stay onGrenade's founders kept a minority stake and kept running it under Mondelēz (Just Food).

            Legal setup in Greece and the EU

            Before your first sale you need nine things in place. Sell in Greece only at first: since August 2026, every extra EU country adds its own packaging registration.

            The biggest legal risk is your name. EU examiners refuse trademarks that describe the product. An obvious misspelling of a descriptive word is not enough to avoid that (CITMA). "Fowm" sounds like "foam", and Fowm makes foam. Pay a trademark attorney for an opinion before you print pouches. If the word alone is too risky, you may need to register the logo, or adjust the name. A buyer will only pay millions for a brand you legally own.

            The nine things, in order

            1. Trademark attorney opinion, then file an EU trademark€850 for 1 class, +€50 for the 2nd, +€150 for each extra (Dury). The attorney costs about €800–1,500 (TMarkMetric). When: now
            2. Hire a food technologist and decide "food, not supplement"Ask for a quote. When: now

            4. AccountantA monthly fee. When: the same day as the company
            5. Register with EFET as a food businessYour technologist files it. When: before your first sale
            6. Packaging registry (EOAN) and a recycling schemeA small yearly fee. When: before your first sale
            7. Legal back label in GreekYour technologist checks it. When: before printing
            8. Website legal pagesTemplates plus your accountant. When: before launch
            9. Each new EU country: VAT, packaging and language rulesCosts vary. When: only once Greece works

            Notes on each step

            Trademark

            File in class 29 and 30 at least (food and coffee products). Search TMview for similar names first. You have 5 years to start using the mark in the EU.

            Food, not supplement

            In Greece, food supplements go through the National Organisation for Medicines (EOF), while foods fall under EFET (EOF). A coffee foam powder fits better as a food. Your technologist confirms it.

            Company

            An IKE protects your personal money if something goes wrong, and it's what buyers and investors expect. Company profits pay 22% tax, and dividends 5%. As the only owner-manager you'll likely also pay social security: the lowest category is €250.77 a month in 2026 (Newmoney). That's why you start the company late, not now. If you're under 18 when you set it up, ask the accountant how a parent signs for you.

            Accountant

            Electronic invoicing to the tax office (myDATA) is mandatory from 2026 (Zunapro). Clean books from day one also make the company easier to sell.

            EFET

            The company named on the label is responsible for the food. Register before you sell anything, even to friends.

            Packaging

            Under Greek law, anyone who packs goods, or has them packed for them, must join the national producer register and a recycling scheme (OENET).

            The label

            Mandatory information must be in a language the buyers understand (European Commission), so Greek for Greece. Milk must be highlighted as an allergen, since whey comes from milk. Online, all mandatory information except the best-before date must be on the product page before people buy (European Commission).

            What you can claim

            "High protein" needs at least 20% of the calories from protein, and "source of protein" 12% (University of Reading). Fowm easily qualifies. Health claims must come word for word from the EU's approved list (European Commission). Never write "burns fat", "boosts metabolism" or anything about weight-loss drugs.

            Selling to other EU countries
            • VAT: below €10,000 a year of sales to other EU countries you charge Greek VAT. Above that, you charge each customer's country VAT (Taxually).
            • Packaging: shipping to Germany requires LUCID registration and a recycling licence. Fines reach €200,000 (Koksal). Foreign online sellers now need an authorised representative in Germany (ecoPV). Since 12 August 2026, even one parcel to France triggers registration there (Endereco).
            • Plan: Greece first. Add one country at a time, through a compliance service, once Greek customers reorder.
